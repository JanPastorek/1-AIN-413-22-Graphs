# Proposed seminar assessment and AI-use policy

For adoption after lecturer approval. This replaces only the old seminar's 30 homework points and 20 group-project points. It does not amend the separate course projects, exam, grade thresholds, or absence regulations.

## Assessment: 50 points

| Evidence | Points | When / individual responsibility |
|---|---:|---|
| Best 8 of 10 exit tickets | 16 | Two points each, typically Weeks 1–10; unseen individual questions in class |
| Two live practical checks | 16 | Eight points each; one routing practical and one coloring/code-review practical |
| Graphathon | 12 | Six points for team work; six for each student's protected transfer answer |
| Two proof/counterexample clinics | 6 | Three points each; degree-sequence and planarity tasks completed individually after exploration |
| Total | 50 | No graded take-home product |

The scheduled practical replaces the ordinary activity in its host meeting. Exploration cards and weekly preparation are for feedback, not twelve additional graded reports. Publish the two practical dates before teaching starts. A student's practical score must be supported by that student's written explanation or observed review, even where exploration is paired.

## Three clear tool zones

| Zone | Rule | Typical work |
|---|---|---|
| Protected | Work individually; no AI, internet, or assistance. Any permitted notes or accessibility support are announced in advance. | Exit tickets, individual practical sections, Graphathon transfer |
| Tool-aware | Use only the tools permitted on the task card. Explain material assistance and verify every claim. | PHOEG/House of Graphs exploration, coding and tests |
| AI critique | An instructor-supplied generated answer is material to analyze, not an authority. | Locate a false implication, repair a proof, design a failing test |

Ungraded preparation may use AI. In a tool-aware coding block, disclose the tool and what it helped with in two sentences; no full chat-history collection is required. Never upload classmates' personal data or confidential assessment material. No paid AI account is required: ordinary local tools are sufficient.

## What we assess

Can you define the model, state the assumptions, justify a step, check a witness, and adapt to a new instance? You may be asked to change a weight, remove an assumption, trace code, or explain why a test is incomplete. Novelty, speed, an attractive visualization, and a high leaderboard position are not substitutes for these abilities.

Errors in a first attempt are part of learning. Feedback should identify a repairable mathematical issue. A provided solution, database invariant, or AI output must be labeled as a source until you have independently justified the part you use.

## Fair participation and review

Pair roles rotate. Equivalent supervised written explanations are available where appropriate; speaking style is not assessed. All physical activities have a seated paper alternative. Unplanned platform failure triggers the offline graph cards, not a penalty.

The best-eight rule absorbs two missed/low exit tickets, but does not replace formal accommodations. The lecturer must approve the make-up procedure and any individual minimum requirements before adoption. Suspected misconduct is handled by the institution's established process, not AI-detector scores.

## Review-time budget

Thirty students at two minutes each require 60 examiner-minutes. The two practicals therefore include a protected written section for everyone and rotating short oral checks; a full individual viva for every student requires additional staffed time. Two 6–8-minute vivas for 30 students would need 6–8 examiner-hours before administration. Do not promise that within a normal seminar without staffing.
