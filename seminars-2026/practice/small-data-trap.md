# Alternative studio — The small-data trap

**90-minute coloring discovery lesson.** Use instead of another lesson when its content has been covered elsewhere, or as a separately scheduled session. Prerequisites: clique, proper coloring, odd cycles, contradiction. Tools are allowed during exploration; the final proof/evidence check is protected.

## A — Foundation

In a conflict graph, edges mean two events cannot share a slot. Does no triangle mean the graph is bipartite? Try an odd cycle before making a general claim. Explain which property bounds the number of pairwise conflicting events and which controls the number of slots.

## B — Core studio

1. Model a schedule with no triple of pairwise conflicting events. Predict whether three slots always suffice.
2. In [PHOEG](https://phoeg.umons.ac.be/phoeg/), select **Clique number** versus **Chromatic number**; constrain clique number at most 2; compare orders 5–8. Use a teacher-prepared view for 9–10 if available. Record the exact scope of any claim you make.
3. Search [House of Graphs](https://houseofgraphs.org/search) for a graph with **Number of Vertices = 11**, **Clique Number = 2**, **Chromatic Number = 4**. Ask for the reveal card if the interface delays the mathematics. Record its graph URL and independently check what you can.
4. The teacher supplies a construction from a five-cycle. Reconstruct its edges and prove it has no triangle. Then complete the provided recoloring contradiction showing why three colors do not suffice. Exhibit an upper-bound coloring too.
5. Rehearse a four-minute explanation to another pair. Each student explains a different step. Repair the argument “no triangles means a greedy algorithm uses at most three colors.”

## C — Challenge

Generalize the supplied construction: what happens to clique-freeness and chromatic number? Or distinguish the unrestricted statement from the theorem about triangle-free **planar** graphs. Do not claim to have proved the latter from finite experiments.

## Timing

0–8 definitions; 8–18 model; 18–32 PHOEG; 32–42 database counterexample; 42–62 construction/proof; 62–75 peer explanation; 75–83 proof repair; 83–90 individual check. Teacher-held reveal cards are not released in this public starter sheet.
