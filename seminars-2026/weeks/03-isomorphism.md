# 03 — Graph forensics

**90 minutes.** Prerequisites: degrees, cycles, components, bijections. Tools: paper first; House of Graphs identity search permitted after prediction.

You will separate drawings from structure, refute an incomplete invariant test, and justify an automorphism.

## A — Foundation

Define an isomorphism as a bijection preserving adjacency in both directions. Which of vertex count, edge count, degree multiset, component count, and chromatic number are invariants? Does preservation of one imply isomorphism?

## B — Core studio

Graph G has vertices 1–6 and edges 12,23,34,45,56,61. Graph H has vertices a–f and edges ac,ce,eb,bd,df,fa. Graph J has edges 12,23,31,45,56,64 on vertices 1–6.

1. Draw each graph in two visually different ways. With a budget of three invariant queries, decide which pairs could be isomorphic.
2. Produce an explicit isomorphism where one exists, and a decisive structural witness where one does not. Check every edge under the proposed map; similar pictures are not enough.
3. Refute “same sorted degrees implies isomorphic.” Then ask whether adding connectedness makes this a reliable test in general. A lack of a counterexample on six vertices is not a proof.
4. Draw G in [House of Graphs](https://houseofgraphs.org/draw_graph), search for its identity, and compare its invariant table with your calculations. No upload is required. Record the individual graph URL if found; a failed identity search does not mean your graph is invalid.
5. Exhibit a nontrivial automorphism of G. Explain the difference between permuting labels arbitrarily and preserving adjacency.
6. Rehearse a four-minute explanation for another pair; each speaker owns a different justification, and the audience asks one assumption question.

## C — Challenge

Find connected non-isomorphic regular graphs with the same order and degree. Or find a small set of vertices of G that, if fixed pointwise, forces every automorphism to be the identity. Distinguish fixing vertices pointwise from merely preserving the set.

## Session rhythm

7 recall; 10 drawing; 20 evidence; 15 query/check; 18 proof/explanation; 13 challenge or revision; 7 protected exit. A printed database record is the fallback. Your explanation must stand independently of the record.
